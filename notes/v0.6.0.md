A new engine under the PC dashboard, a record of every trouble-code clear, and an update button.

**Windows program rebuilt in Rust:** the same dashboard and pages, now a single native program of 3.7 MB (was 10 MB). Your drives, layouts, phone pairings and settings in `Documents\OBD Listener` are used as
they are. If OBD Listener is already running, opening it again brings up the running one in your browser.

**Code-clear tracking:** clearing trouble codes erases the codes, the freeze frame and the self-test results, so
every clear is now kept:
- Clearing from the app (PC, head unit or phone) reads and saves the codes first and marks the moment in the drive.
- Clears made with any other tool (a garage scanner, another app) are recognised in your recordings from the car's
  own status, even between two drives.
- They show as a marker on the drive's charts, in a new **Clear history** on Diagnostics, on Car health and as a
  badge on Drives.
- Switching the engine off to clear codes is no longer counted as a stall.

**Update button:** Settings & about (PC), the head unit's settings panel and the phone screen can check for a new
version and install it on a tap. Nothing updates by itself; on the head unit the button sits in the top part of
the screen you can touch. The first time, Android asks you to allow OBD Dash to install apps.

**Fixes:** the Drives page could fail to open, and switching pages quickly could leave the previous page showing.

Install over 0.5.x / 0.4.x (same signing key); your drives, pairings and settings are kept.
